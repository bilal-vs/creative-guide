# Verdant Soft: social strategy (handler's view)

> **Status: PROPOSAL v1, 2026-10-04.** Revised after the audit (`brand/research.md` → "Audit results"; raw evidence in `brand/audit-raw.md`). The user approves it next. Once approved, its rules move into `STYLE_GUIDE.md` §3 and §11. Until then, nothing here is binding on the pipeline.

It's written the way a social media handler thinks: **past** (what the brand has already said and shown), **present** (where it stands and what's holding it back), **future** (what to post, why, and how it grows).

### What changed from v0
1. **Visual world.** It now follows the real brand: a **slate blue → sage teal gradient** (#416D95 → #74AFAD) on off-white and ink, set in Inter. **It is not green.** The v0 idea of "green, organic growing forms" is gone. Growth is now shown through structure and light (§3).
2. **Continuity call.** The website is the brand's best expression, so social moves onto it. The past social templates (royal navy with yellow capitals, AI robots, stock people) are dropped on purpose (§1).
3. **Insight posts** must come from Verdant Soft's own projects. The generic insight posts they've run got the least engagement (§2, §6).
4. **Real-people posts** do best, and the pipeline must never fake people. So the news inbox matters more, and inbox items about people jump the queue (§8).
5. **Proof** now has six quotable entries from five clients, plus ten case studies. Thursday alternates **Client words** and **Project spotlight** (§6, §7).
6. **No self-reported numbers** until the user confirms one set. The numbers on the site and on LinkedIn disagree (§10).
7. **Captions get a reset:** 3–5 hashtags instead of 10–16, no Unicode bold, a hook first instead of emoji (§5).
8. **The questions** at the end are refreshed (§14).

## 1. Past: what exists already
- **Website** (verdant-soft.com).
  - **Look:** calm and minimal, premium-leaning SaaS. Off-white panels (#F9F9F9), ink headlines (#1B1B1B), Inter, and a few words per headline in the blue → teal gradient. A fine line-and-node mesh in the hero, and an oversized faint VS mark in the footer.
  - **Copy:** accurate but generic ("innovative", "seamless", "empower"). A few lines are genuinely good:
    - "Your brand deserves better. Let's build it right."
    - "No guesswork, just a clear path from ideas → results."
    - "We're full-cycle strategy, design, development, deployment"
   - "Smarter decisions start with clear answers."
- **What it sells:** four services, each with a six-step process. Outsourcing comes as dedicated, project-based, managed or offshore teams. Payment is milestone-based. There are ten case studies (healthcare, e-commerce, real estate, parking, VPN, dental, Shopify sync, a design canvas, ETL), all without client names.
- **Proof:** a verified Clutch review (AlgoRift / Sweet Round, Australia; 4.5 overall, 5.0 for quality, schedule, cost and referral), five website testimonials that are safe to quote, and one more (Elia Essen) held until the client is confirmed. Every one praises the same things:
  - ownership
  - communication
  - understanding the problem before building
  - delivering on time
  - value
- **Tagline:** "Engineering Tomorrow’s Tech Today!" lives on LinkedIn, Instagram and Clutch, not on the website.
- **Social so far** (background; the audit was narrowed to business info):
  - **LinkedIn:** about 10K followers. 19 posts in 11 months with long gaps. Typical post: about 17 reactions.
  - **What works there:** real people and events (new hires, Mango Day, an expo) at 29–40 reactions.
  - **What doesn't:** generic insight posts at 4–11 reactions.
  - **The look:** no system. Mostly royal navy with white and yellow capitals, plus one-offs, AI robots and stock people.
  - **Instagram (@verdant_soft):** 146 followers. Mostly office-humour Reels with real staff, a local and talent audience.

**Continuity decisions** (changing the brand on purpose, not by accident):

| Element | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Logo, VS mark, blue → teal gradient | **Keep, always** | Consistent across the site, avatars and banner. It's what people recognise |
| Website system (off-white, ink, Inter, gradient words, mesh, cropped VS mark) | **Keep → becomes the social system** | The best expression of the brand; social should look like the company buyers land on |
| Royal navy + yellow templates, all-caps shouts | **Drop** | Not brand colours. They clash with the site and logo, and perform no better |
| AI robots, glowing generic tech, stock "staff" | **Drop** | Clichés, and they undercut "real team, real work" |
| Real-people posts (hires, culture, events, campus) | **Keep**, through the news inbox | Best engagement. The pipeline can't fake them |
| Insight posts | **Fix**: ground them in their own projects | The generic versions get the least engagement |
| Captions (emoji openers, Unicode bold, 10–16 hashtags) | **Fix** | They read as template content, and Unicode bold breaks screen readers and search |
| Claims beyond the evidence ("redefined how clinics manage…", "Marketing" listed as a service) | **Fix** | Buyers check claims. Scope words come from the case study; services come only from F02 |
| Tagline | **Keep** in bios and banner; rare in posts | It's already established there |
| Instagram office-humour Reels | **Leave to humans** | Real staff on video; outside the pipeline |

## 2. Present: diagnosis
1. **Sounds like everyone.** Thousands of offshore development shops say "innovative, scalable, seamless". So does Verdant Soft's own site copy and LinkedIn Overview. A buyer scrolling the feed can't tell them apart.
2. **The buyer's real question is trust, not capability.** Founders hiring a remote team worry about:
   - missed deadlines
   - poor communication and time-zone silence
   - low code quality
   - developers disappearing mid-project
   - lock-in and losing ownership of the work
3. **Verdant Soft's proof answers those fears in the clients' own words.** The quotes are all in `STYLE_GUIDE.md` §2 `PUBLIC-PROOF-v2`:
   - "took full ownership" (P02, pending confirmation)
   - "proactive communication"
   - "quickly understood our problem"
   - "sought a clear understanding before starting the work"
   - "consistently delivering milestones on time"
   - "maximizing value while managing costs"
4. **There's an underused differentiator: full cycle under one roof.** UX research → design → build (web, mobile, AI SaaS) → cloud and DevOps → a dedicated team. The site says it ("We're full-cycle"), and each service has a defined process.
5. **The name is a gift. The colour is the brand.** *Verdant* means growing. The ownable visual is the slate-blue → teal gradient and the VS mark, not literal green.
6. **Social is a credibility check.** Buyers find Verdant Soft through online search, Upwork, Fiverr or LinkedIn outreach; the Clutch reviewer came from "Online Search". Then they look at the page. The feed has to make them think: *real team, real work, knows what it's doing, still active.*
7. **The feed doesn't look like the company.** Social and the website use different colours, fonts and moods. A buyer who clicks from a post to the site sees two brands.
8. **Real beats generic.** Posts with real people and real events get about 5× the reactions of generic tips. The automation can't make people posts, so its own content (insight, how we work, proof) has to be specific, first-hand and useful, or it won't earn attention.
9. **The numbers don't agree.** The homepage, a LinkedIn post and Clutch give counts that contradict each other. A careful buyer will notice. Until one set is confirmed, posts state no numbers.
10. **Company-page reach is low.** 10K followers, and a typical post gets about 17 reactions. Steady daily posting helps, and staff reshares help more (§12).

**Creative platform: "Growth, engineered."** This is internal, not a public tagline; the public tagline stays "Engineering Tomorrow’s Tech Today!". It's one idea that serves all three goals:

| Goal | What "growth, engineered" means |
|---|---|
| Clients | Products built to scale: architecture, cloud and process that hold up as the business grows |
| Brand | A clear point of view on building software well: practical, specific, opinionated |
| Talent | Careers that grow: real international projects, modern stack, learning |

**Visual world (revised; final rules come from Phase 1 tests):** *structure that grows, in the brand's light.*
- **Palette.** Strictly the website palette (`STYLE_GUIDE.md` §5.1): slate blue #416D95 → sage teal #74AFAD for light and accents, on off-white #F9F9F9 (default) or ink #1B1B1B (contrast days).
- **Subjects.** Growth shown through form: modular blocks assembling, layered translucent panels rising, branching node networks (an echo of the site's mesh), light moving through architecture, precise physical models of systems.
- **Rendering.** Clean, calm, lots of negative space for overlays. Soft studio or architectural light. It should feel like the website brought to life.
- **Brand assets stay deterministic.** The logo, the cropped VS mark, headlines and gradient words are overlaid after generation, never generated.

**Clichés to avoid:**
- a plant sprouting from a laptop, literal leaves or green
- lightbulbs, coins and growth arrows
- handshakes
- robots and humanoid AI, glowing brains
- generic "hacker in hoodie"
- stock-style smiling teams
- floating holographic icon clouds
- all-caps shout layouts
- yellow accents

## 5. Voice
**Engineer-to-founder:** confident, plain-spoken, specific, warm. It sounds like a senior engineer you'd trust with your product, not an agency brochure. The site's own line is the north star: **"Let's build it right."**

- **Do:**
  - one idea per post
  - concrete nouns and real tech names (from `FACTS`)
  - short sentences
  - show the process
  - admit trade-offs
  - end with a useful takeaway or a clear, low-pressure CTA (book a meeting, or verdant-soft.com/hire-us)
- **Don't:**
  - buzzwords: innovative, cutting-edge, seamless, leverage, synergy, game-changer, unlock, revolutionise, delve, empower, top-tier, "in today's fast-paced world"
  - hype, or more than one exclamation mark
  - emoji walls
  - invented numbers, or any number not `confirmed` in `FACTS`
  - posts that make it all about "us" and give the reader nothing

**From the past feed → to the new voice:**

| Past habit | New rule |
|---|---|
| Emoji as the first character ("🚀 Exciting opportunity!") | The first line is a hook in words. Emoji are optional: max 2, never first on LinkedIn |
| Unicode "bold" letters (𝗟𝗲𝘁’𝘀) | Plain text only (accessibility and search) |
| 10–16 hashtags, dot-spacer lines | 3–5 hashtags at the end, always including #VerdantSoft |
| Generic claims ("top-tier IT solutions") | One specific thing: a real project, a real decision, a real client sentence |
| "We are pleased to announce…" | Lead with what the reader gets |

- **Platform register:**
  - **LinkedIn:** fuller and insight-led. 120–220 words, line breaks, hook before "see more".
  - **Instagram:** shorter and visual-led. 40–100 words, punchy first line.

## 6. Content pillars
| # | Pillar | Goal | What it covers | Share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Engineering insight** | Brand / thought leadership | One practical lesson **tied to Verdant Soft's own work**: a decision from a case study (multi-tenant ETL, zone-based parking sessions, Shopify ↔ Vue sync, a Konva canvas), or their stack (F05–F06). No generic listicles | ~29% |
| 2 | **How we work** | Clients | Takes the risk out of outsourcing: milestone payments (F12), engagement models (F13), single-service requests (F15), the six-step processes, communication, handover, ownership | ~29% |
| 3 | **Proof** | Clients | Alternates **Client words** (a verbatim quote from `PUBLIC-PROOF-v2`) and **Project spotlight** (one case study, F30–F39, no client named). **Max 1 per week; the same `client_key` not again within 30 days** | ~14% |
| 4 | **Grow with us** | Talent | **Inbox first:** real hires, events, campus drives, culture days (real photos). Otherwise conceptual posts on learning, stack and what the work is like. **No AI-generated "employees"** | ~14% |

**Format for v1: one post per day, cross-posted.** The same image goes to both platforms, with **separate captions** per platform. That halves the generation and judging cost, keeps the brand consistent, and fits the 4:5 format on both. Platform-specific posts are a v2 option.

**Weekly rotation (draft):**

| Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Insight | How we work | Insight | Proof\* | Grow with us | Brand world | How we work (light) |

\* Thursday alternates Client words and Project spotlight. If no proof item is eligible (because of the caps), Thursday falls back to *How we work*.

## 7. Recurring series (memory and continuity)
Series give the feed a rhythm, give followers a reason to come back, and give the automation a clear "what's next". Episodes are numbered and tracked in `posts/index.md`.

| Series | Pillar | Idea | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Build Notes** | Insight | One real engineering decision per episode, drawn from a case study or the stack (F05–F06, F30–F39). The problem, the choice, the trade-off | Open-ended |
| **Outsourcing, Decoded** | How we work | One buyer fear per episode, answered with real facts: how payments work (F12), what if I don't know what I need, can I hire for one service (F15), changes after launch, time zones, code ownership, handover | ~12 episodes, then refreshed |
| **Wireframe → Production** | How we work | Their real processes, one step per episode: UI/UX six steps (F04), then the custom-software and DevOps steps | Finite (~12–18), can re-run yearly |
| **Project Spotlight** | Proof | One case study per episode (F30–F39), no client named | 10 episodes, then refreshed as new case studies arrive |
| **Grow at Verdant** | Talent | Skills, stack, intern advice, what the work is like | Open-ended |

## 8. Inputs: how the handler decides what to post today
Priority order. The first one that applies wins.
1. **News inbox:** real things the team supplies, such as a launch, a new hire, a campus drive, a culture day (Mango Day), an expo, a new review, or a milestone. Real photos are allowed only from here.
   - **Inbox items with real people take the day** whatever the rotation says, because they perform best. Max one per day; the rest queue.
   - The format will be defined in §11 of the guide, as a folder or sheet the pipeline reads.
2. **Calendar moment** for today (see below).
3. **Series episode** due according to the rotation.
4. **Evergreen** topic from the pillar's topic bank, respecting no-repeat windows.

Hiring posts use **only roles supplied through the news inbox.** The careers page's job list was broken during the audit, and the pipeline never invents roles.

## 10. Guardrails (proposed)
- **Facts:** state only facts from the fact bank (`STYLE_GUIDE.md` §2 `FACTS`) marked `confirmed`. Never invent metrics, clients, awards, years or team size.
- **Numbers:** no self-reported numbers (project counts, client counts, satisfaction rates, user reach, savings percentages) until the user confirms them (F50–F52 are pending). Never state team size or headcount.
- **Proof:** quote only `quote_verbatim` or `alt_quotes` text from `PUBLIC-PROOF`, character for character, with the exact `attribution`. Max 1 proof post per week. The same `client_key` not again within 30 days. Never print star ratings.
- **Case studies:** never name the client. Keep the scope words ("built the … modules", "contributed to"); never inflate them ("redefined…"). Never quote the case-study "The Process" boilerplate.
- **Services:** name only the four services in F02. Never "Marketing" or "Business Development", which past graphics listed.
- **People:** never present AI-generated people as Verdant Soft staff, clients or events. Talent and culture visuals are conceptual, or use people who can't be identified (hands, silhouettes, shot from behind, out of focus).
- **No famous-people quotes or likenesses** (e.g. the past Mark Zuckerberg quote card).
- **Culture and religion:** use only the pre-approved templates for national and religious days. Never generate Arabic or Urdu script, religious figures, or sacred sites.
- **Brands:** no third-party logos or trademarks in images. Tech names in captions are fine.
- **Identity:** never confuse the company with other "Verdant" companies.

## 11. Posting time
The buyers are abroad, so optimise for **their** mornings, not Lahore's:
- Central Europe 08:00–10:00 CET = about 12:00–14:00 PKT
- US East 08:00–10:00 ET = about 18:00–20:00 PKT
- Australia East 08:00–10:00 AEST = about 03:00–05:00 PKT (new: the one verified Clutch client is in Brisbane)

**Proposal:** LinkedIn at about 13:00 PKT on weekdays, and Instagram at about 19:00 PKT, which also suits local talent in the evening. If Australia turns out to be a core market, test an early-morning LinkedIn slot. To be confirmed in the guide.
