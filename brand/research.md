## Audit results

### A. The business, as the website presents it
- **What they sell:** four services: Custom Software Development · Cloud & DevOps · IT Team Outsourcing · UI/UX Design.
  - Each service page has a six-step process and a tools list.
  - Outsourcing comes as dedicated teams, project-based outsourcing, managed IT services or offshore development, on full-time, part-time or project-based terms.
  - Payment is **milestone-based**: "at project initiation, key development phases, and final delivery" (FAQ).
- **Calls to action:** "Book a Meeting" (opens a Calendly booking) · "Hire an Expert" (`/hire-us`) · "Get in Touch" (`/contact-us`).
- **Proof of work:** ten case studies, all **without client names**:
  - psychiatric clinic and hospital system (they built specific modules)
  - e-commerce CMS
  - real estate platform
  - zone-based parking app
  - VPN browser extension and subscriptions
  - dental care and learning platform
  - Shopify ↔ Vue.js sync
  - clinic management
  - browser-based design canvas (Vue.js + Konva.js)
  - multi-tenant ETL platform (they "contributed")

  The industries cluster around **healthcare**, **e-commerce** and **SaaS/platforms**.
- **The brand's own best lines:**
  - "Your brand deserves better. Let's build it right." (footer)
  - "No guesswork, just a clear path from ideas → results."
  - "We're full-cycle strategy, design, development, deployment" (blog)
  - "We don’t just write code we solve problems." (blog)
  - "software should work for you, not the other way around." (blog)
  - "Smarter decisions start with clear answers." (FAQ heading)

  Most of the rest is generic ("innovative", "seamless", "visually stunning", "empower").
- **What clients praise**, in their own words:
  - **ownership:** "took full ownership of our AI SaaS platform"
  - **communication:** "proactive communication", "positive communication", "Their response time was also amazing"
  - **understanding before building:** "quickly understood our problem", "sought a clear understanding before starting the work"
  - **on time:** "consistently delivering milestones on time", "completed the work in a timely manner"
  - **value:** "maximizing value while managing costs"

  This matches the positioning in `brand/strategy.md`.
- **Credibility risks a buyer could spot:**
  - **Numbers that don't agree.** The homepage says "500+ Successful Projects", "36+ Active Clients", "95% Client satisfaction rate" and "5+ Years of expertise". A January 2026 LinkedIn post says "50+ Scalable & Secure Solutions", "1M+ Global Users Reached" and "69+ Websites Launched", plus a team-size figure that contradicts Clutch's range. A blog post says "up to 40%" lower cloud costs, with no client named.
  - **Copied boilerplate.** Every case study has the same "The Process" text, which refers to "The Polish team" and "the San Francisco team".
  - **Typos on the site.** "Alogirft", "Developement", "Prometheous", "Powsershell", "After Affects", "verity", "Something when wrong", "We follows", "Verdant Soft bring".
  - **A broken careers job list.**
  - **Overclaims and drift.** A LinkedIn post says "We have redefined how psychiatric clinics and hospitals manage their operations", but the case study says they built four modules. The one Clutch project had "1 Employee" assigned, while the site quotes the same client calling them "a team". A LinkedIn graphic lists "Marketing" and "Business Development" as services, but the site offers neither.
  - **Voice slips.** The outsourcing intro is written in the third person ("They offer…"). An FAQ answer switches to "I'll be happy to assist you". The LinkedIn Overview and the Clutch description are the same generic paragraph.
  - **Thin testimonials.** Five of the seven written ones show no title or company, and the video testimonials aren't transcribed.
- **Fact checks:**
  - **Tagline:** "Engineering Tomorrow’s Tech Today!" appears on LinkedIn, Instagram and Clutch, but **not on the website**.
  - **Founded 2019:** stated only on Clutch. It fits the site's "5+ Years".
  - **"Alogirft":** a website typo for **AlgoRift** (see §5).

### B. Visual identity (the website is the reference)
- **Colours.** The brand colour is a **slate blue → sage teal gradient**, `linear-gradient(102.32deg, #416D95, #74AFAD)`.
  - The CSS calls it `--color-green-gradient`, but **it is not green**.
  - The site uses it on the logo and on highlighted words in headlines.
  - The rest of the palette is neutral: ink #1B1B1B (headings, footer, dark buttons), body grey #5E5E5E, secondary grey #707070, light panels #F9F9F9 and white #FFFFFF.
  - The logo files use exactly this gradient: sampled ends are #416D95 and about #77B1AE, within ΔE00 ≤ 1.1 of the CSS values. The "#406090 / #307080" readings in `audit-raw.md` §2 came from semi-transparent pixels and are not real colours.
  - Seen but not part of the palette: rating-star yellow #FDC700, icon black, and #406C94 and #26615E (one element each, purpose unknown).
- **Type:** **Inter** everywhere, self-hosted.
  - Headlines 700 at 48px; section headings 600; body 500/400; letter-spacing normal.
  - Signature move: a few words in each headline set in the gradient, the rest in ink.
- **Logo:** a "VS" monogram (a looped V stroke) plus the "Verdant Soft" wordmark.
  - Variants: gradient lockup for light backgrounds, white lockup for dark backgrounds, white vector mark, gradient mark.
  - Only the white mark is true vector. The lockups are PNGs wrapped in SVG files; the white one is 4096 px wide, the gradient one 852 px.
  - There is no `og:image`.
- **Imagery and motifs:**
  - A lot of off-white space, with rounded #F9F9F9 panels.
  - Fine grey-blue line-and-node mesh illustrations (hero).
  - An oversized, cropped, faint VS mark as a background shape (footer).
  - Darkened, greyscale photo tiles for the case studies.
  - Stock photos on the blog cards.

  The overall feel: calm, minimal, premium-leaning SaaS.

### Keep / Fix / Drop / Gaps
**Keep**
1. **Logo and VS mark, plus the slate-blue → sage-teal gradient.** They're consistent across the site, the LinkedIn avatar and banner, and the Instagram avatar. This is the recognisable core.
2. **The website's visual system** as the basis for social templates: off-white and ink, Inter, gradient-highlighted words, the line-and-node mesh, and the oversized cropped VS mark.
3. **The tagline "Engineering Tomorrow’s Tech Today!"** in bios and banners, where it already lives. Rarely in posts.
4. **Real-people posts:** new hires, culture days, events, campus drives. They are the best performers. Keep them, supplied by humans through the news inbox, because the pipeline must not fake people.
5. **The brand's own lines**, "Let's build it right." and "No guesswork, just a clear path from ideas → results.", as voice anchors.
6. **Proof.** Five quotable website testimonials plus a verified Clutch review: six entries from five clients. Their themes match the positioning (ownership, communication, understanding first, on time, value).
7. **Ten case studies** as raw material for project spotlights and engineering insight.
8. **#VerdantSoft** as the one constant hashtag. **Document carousels** as a format (already used; v2 of the guide).

**Fix**
1. **The feed doesn't look like the brand.** Move social from the royal navy and yellow templates to the website palette and Inter.
2. **Captions:**
   - Cut 10–16 hashtags to 3–5.
   - No Unicode bold: screen readers garble it and search can't read it.
